I visually inspected all 51 main slides in order in `thesis-defense-overleaf-handout.pdf`, without reading source, speaker notes, manuscripts, other reviews, or outside references. The initial assignment also requested backup inspection, which I completed before receiving the instruction to assess only the main talk. This report excludes backup-based findings and does not use backups to repair main-slide omissions. Numbers below are main-slide numbers. This is an audience review, not certification of technical correctness.

**Verdict: the complete main argument is not reliably followable without oral rescue.** The two research questions are clear. The particle-filter construction can largely be reconstructed from the slides. The main break is slides 12–21: a growing policy class, stale-policy optimism, rare critic changes, and resets are introduced faster than their causal links are established. The final planning section has a separate scope/status ambiguity.

## Reconstruction from the main slides

**Opening, 1–3.** Actions affect the data collected; hidden states complicate likelihood inference. The talk develops these as two sequential-learning problems and then considers their connection to action choice.

**Actor–critic, 4–25.** We observe states and choose actions over H-step episodes to maximize expected total reward. The critic regresses immediate reward plus estimated continuation value. The actor raises probabilities of high-valued actions. Regression on visited states does not ensure exploration, so an optimistic critic takes the largest value consistent with regression data. The theoretical obstacle is that policy-evaluation targets depend on an actor accumulating past critics, making uniform control of the possible regression targets increasingly complex. Infrequent critic changes alone leave a critic optimistic for an old policy that can lag behind the improving actor. The repair uses optimality targets, rare critic changes triggered by excess regression loss, and uniform actor resets. Under representation and complexity assumptions, the displayed regret guarantee has square-root dependence on episode count, with horizon and complexity factors. Tetris favors Algorithm 1, not the reset-based Algorithm 2. Offline data can permit variants without optimistic optimization under a coverage/sample-size condition. AntMaze favors Algorithm 2H at the endpoint, but its neural implementations approximate optimism.

**Differentiable particle filtering, 26–46.** We want likelihood-based parameter estimates in a hidden-state model whose transitions can be simulated. A particle filter predicts states, weights by observation density, and resamples. Parameter-dependent resampling obstructs direct differentiation. The construction runs a baseline filter at phi, reuses its simulator draws and ancestor indices while varying theta, and corrects ancestor-selection weights using measurement-density ratios. Differentiating the corrected likelihood estimate at theta = phi gives a score estimate. Discounting older corrections by alpha trades score bias against particle variability. The MSE bound requires regularity and derivative stability beyond state mixing; fixed-N consistency is stated at alpha = 1. Dhaka plots illustrate a recovery-rate bias–variance tradeoff and higher attained likelihoods for warm-start-plus-gradient optimization with alpha = .97 in a matched-time comparison.

**Inference to control, 47–50.** Hidden velocity motivates a distribution over states. Full belief planning branches on future observations. QMDP assumes future full observation; a short belief tree delays that approximation. Slide 49 combines learning, filtering, and planning while distinguishing depth-d analysis from d = 0 experiments. No control guarantee or empirical result is shown. I therefore read this as a connection/next-problem illustration rather than a demonstrated consequence of the previous results. Its status is not explicit enough on the slide.

## Highest-priority issues

1. **12–13: explain the statistical obstacle before its covering-number algebra.** “Their sum need not lie in F” does not explain why estimation becomes harder. Slide 13 then introduces a policy covering number, beta_T, and a regret expression. The reason uniform concentration must cover adaptively constructed regression targets is confined to the small footer. A statistics audience can understand this, but needs that link first. **Minimal repair:** connect the actor in slide 8's regression response to the family of possible responses in one main-body sentence, define Pi_T as the possible learned policies, then show K-fold complexity. Keep “a vacuous general bound, not an impossibility result” next to the conclusion so it is not mistaken for proof of linear algorithmic regret.

2. **15: the stale-critic diagram overloads the reader.** It simultaneously shows current-policy value, optimal value, an optimistic Q-star critic, its greedy-policy values, stale Q-pi critics, update times, several shaded regions, and multiple superscript conventions. The Q-star construction is introduced only on slide 16. “Rust area” is difficult to distinguish amid overlapping pink/blue shading. **Minimal repair:** keep the current-policy curve, stale-policy critic, update times, and one labeled gap. Introduce optimality critics on slide 16. This is actual confusion, not merely a preference for simpler artwork.

3. **19–21: show the update mechanism before its cost bound, and connect the regression classes.** Slide 19 uses K = O(d H log T) before slide 20 explains when a critic changes. Slide 20 does not identify the class used for its “best regression loss” comparator; slide 21 introduces auxiliary class G after the earlier regression used F. **Minimal repair:** present the trigger first, identify the comparator class, then state that the analysis bounds how often this test fails. Give d's role before using the K bound. The full formal complexity definition need not interrupt the main talk.

4. **17, 21: material scope is visually subordinate.** Slide 17's first inequality depends on a confidence-set event, mentioned only in the footer. Slide 21's “Confidence-set optimization may be computationally difficult” is also small. Given the thesis title's “statistical and computational efficiency,” this result can easily be read as a practical computational guarantee too. **Minimal repair:** attach “on the confidence event” to the inequality and put the sample-efficiency/computational qualification beside the result. The talk also never explains the benchmark behind “Optimal Sample Efficiency” in the paper title. Emphasize the demonstrated square-root-in-T result rather than inventing a lower-bound comparison.

5. **23–24: distinguish hybrid theory from the plotted implementation.** Slide 23 concludes with “variants without optimistic optimization.” The next experiment says, in fine print, that both neural variants approximate optimism by action sampling. AntMaze can therefore be mistaken for a direct evaluation of the optimism-free construction. **Minimal repair:** label slide 24 as a neural implementation comparison and promote the action-sampling qualification. On slide 23, explain that the offline data must control errors relevant to the optimal policy, rather than leaving “bounded coverage” unexplained.

6. **34–41: state differentiability when the construction uses it.** Slide 28 promises simulation without transition-density evaluation. Slide 34 asserts “Paths are differentiable”; smooth simulator and measurement operations arrive on slide 41. Fixing ancestors does not itself explain that premise. **Minimal repair:** add “with a differentiable simulator and measurement density” on slide 34. Keep the stronger derivative-stability conditions on slide 41, where they deserve main-talk attention.

7. **35–39: motivate discounting before inserting alpha.** Alpha first appears as an unexplained weight exponent, four slides before its purpose. Slides 36–37's cancellation and nonzero-derivative explanations are useful. **Minimal repair:** initially show the undiscounted correction, or say on slide 35 that discounting older corrections controls variability at a bias cost. On slide 38, explain that the denominator normalizes the carried correction weights; currently its purpose must be inferred.

8. **42–45: empirical context is insufficient to assess the evidence's strength.** Slide 42's legend says “True Log-Likelihood,” while its footer says “reference likelihood”; its construction is unspecified. Slide 43 omits particle count, Monte Carlo repetitions, and the reference-score definition. Slide 45 gives 100 searches and matched wall-clock budgets, but not the budget or counts of omitted results below -3800. **Minimal repair:** use one consistent reference label and supply essential comparison settings outside the plots. Preserve “in this comparison”; the plots do not establish universal superiority or a universally good alpha.

9. **47–50: clarify the planning section's status.** Slides 47–48 motivate beliefs clearly. Slide 49 abruptly adds MCTQ, MCTS, model learning, fully observed Q, and a small multi-loop diagram. “Analysis: Depth-d belief tree” versus “Experiments: d = 0” gives no result and does not explain what d = 0 removes. Slide 50 calls this the “Next problem,” while slide 49 cites a named NeurIPS 2026 work. **Minimal repair:** label this section's role explicitly, explain that d = 0 omits belief-tree lookahead, and distinguish a conceptual connection from a result established in this talk. If no control result is to be defended here, simplify the diagram to model → belief → action without introducing another algorithm vocabulary.

## Other slide-order observations

- **1–9:** the horizon, Q/V definitions, regression response, and multiplicative update form an accessible progression. Slide 9 is mathematically busy, but the odds identity explains the operation. No wholesale rewrite is needed. The requested title-subtitle update is not a substantive audience concern.
- **10–11:** optimism and regret have useful diagrams. “Consistent with Bellman regression data” is acceptable as an initial abstraction if the later algorithm closes the loop. The fixed-initial-state qualification is easy to miss in slide 11's footer.
- **14, 16, 18:** the changing target, average-to-maximum substitution, and reset motivation are clear. On slide 18, “Before critic change” beside f(s,a2) > f(s,a1) is temporally ambiguous: identify f as the new critic. Put the probability label on the bars rather than only in the footer.
- **22:** the unfavorable Algorithm 2 result is stated candidly. “Shaded bands are reproduced from the paper” does not define their statistical meaning. A brief Tetris reward/episode description and explanation of the regret reference would help interpret the scale.
- **25–33:** the transition is explicit. The simulate–weight–resample sequence is particularly effective for this audience. Slide 27 has more rate symbols than the later argument uses, but that is secondary.
- **36–38:** preserve the selection-probability cancellation and “unit weight, nonzero derivative” explanation. They make the estimator much less mysterious.
- **40–41:** the MSE decomposition and fixed-N consistency have readable scope. Put “observations fixed; expectation over particle-filter randomness” beside the MSE rather than in a footer. “A2, A3, A5, and either A6 or A6-J” is manuscript bookkeeping with little value to a cold listener.
- **44–46:** the fresh-filter optimization loop is understandable. Adam only needs a gloss as the gradient optimizer. The final arrow to action choice must not be mistaken for an established control guarantee.
- **50–51:** the two-column summary is useful and the acknowledgments are unobjectionable.

**Language/taste, separate from confusion:** There is no pervasive cute or quippy phrasing. Most titles describe questions or mechanisms. “Two topics,” “Fitting the model,” and “Filtering and planning” are generic, but changing them is optional. The substantive wording problem is repeated use of small gray footers for assumptions, scope, and experimental qualifications. The deck is dense in places because diagrams, equations, and qualification paragraphs compete—not because the prose is flamboyant.

**Revision priority:** Repair the 12–21 causal chain first, separate hybrid theory from the implementation comparison second, and clarify slide 49's status. Then fix assumption timing and experimental context. These changes would help more than adding equations or compressed summaries.
