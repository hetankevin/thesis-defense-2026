## Substantive remaining fixes

1. **Slide 43: distinguish measured deviation from zero from verified score bias.** The slide says “Score vector at fitted parameters,” “Reference score: zero, as at a stationary point,” and “bias dominates,” while the footer identifies the plotted quantities as the estimated second moment of the score and squared norm of its estimated mean. Those are MSE and squared bias relative to the true score only if that score is zero. The slides do not establish that the fitted parameters are an exact stationary point of the likelihood. A statistics audience is likely to notice this distinction. **Minimal fix:** explicitly label the plotted error and mean as relative to the zero-score reference, and make the stationary-point interpretation conditional. For example, “Deviation from a zero-score reference” and “Squared mean dominates near alpha = 0.” Do not claim the empirical plot validates bias relative to an independently known true score unless that evidence is supplied. This is a material evidence-labeling issue, not taste.

2. **Slides 40–43: make the change of estimator visible in the experiment transition.** Slide 38 builds an after-resampling estimate; slide 41 specifies multinomial resampling. Slide 42's footer now says “Before-resampling illustration with systematic resampling; distinct from the after-resampling theorem.” This is an important qualification, but it is the only place where the audience is told the experiment changes estimator/resampling scheme. Slide 43 does not say which version it plots. **Minimal fix:** promote a short “illustration uses the before-resampling implementation” line on 42, and identify 43's implementation. Its relevance is to illustrate the mechanism/tradeoff, while the exact theorem remains tied to the estimator and conditions already stated. The main talk need not derive both implementations, but it should not invite a direct theorem-verification reading.

3. **Slide 49: the connection is clearer, but its role remains slightly underdetermined.** The joint-work attribution and explicit “d = 0: no belief-tree lookahead” repair the earlier confusion. Nevertheless, “Analysis” and “Experiments” still introduce a third system without saying what result the audience should retain. The diagram's MCTQ label and fully observed Q superscripts are also visually small. **Minimal fix:** label this a “Connection overview” or give one plain sentence explaining that this slide shows how model fitting, state inference, and action choice fit together. That is supported by the slide and enough to make the transition into “Two contributions” on 50 natural. This is lower priority than the empirical-labeling issues above; do not add a new control theorem or experimental claim merely to fill the gap.
